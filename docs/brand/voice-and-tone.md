# Accountability Guild — Voice & Tone

**Status:** Ratified 2026-07-20 (Grant). The verbal companion to `brand-standards.md`. Read before writing marketing copy, product UI strings, Field notes articles, social posts, or emails.

## The one-liner

Editorial, not motivational. Accountability Guild states what happened and what it costs: plainly, truthfully, without cheerleading or apology. Structure beats willpower; the copy just keeps the ledger.

## Voice (constant)

- **Second person, direct.** "Set the standard." "Run the cycle." "Close with receipts." You address the member or the group, not a crowd.
- **Short, declarative sentences.** No em dashes; periods, colons, and semicolons carry the load. "Receipts, not promises."
- **Concrete over abstract.** Real names, real numbers, real amounts: "Brandon kept four of five. $25 owed to the room." Never "boost your productivity."
- **Plain, not clever.** No jokes, no puns, no wordplay, no exclamation points. Emphasis comes from an italic serif span, not punctuation or volume.

## Tone (varies by context)

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Product UI | Neutral, instructional, terse. Labels and states, not sentences where a label will do. |
| Marketing site | Confident, editorial, matter-of-fact. Headlines state the mechanic; the italic serif carries the one beat of feeling. |
| Field notes (blog) | Explanatory, a little more room to breathe, still plain. Teaches the rhythm; cites real research (see behavior-science KB). |
| Founder content | Brandon's own voice. Social and ads speak as "we" (Brandon and his group), not "I" (Brandon, 2026-10-02 for the Reddit founder ad; 2026-10-12 for founder long-form posts). "I" stays available for signed founder pieces (e.g. a Field notes letter) and is the only place it appears. |
| Charge / receipt emails | Especially plain and exact. Never joke about money. State the amount, the reason, the destination. |

## Money language (decided 2026-07-20)

**Factual, truthful, with ownership — semi-blunt.** The forfeit mechanic is the product; never soften it into vagueness, never wink at it.

- State amounts directly: "Miss twice, $50 to the kitty." Not "small stakes may apply."
- Own it honestly: the money is real, the charge is automatic, it goes to the group's kitty, never the company. Say so plainly.
- Not glib. Avoid "feed the kitty," "cough up," or humor that undercuts trust around a real charge.
- Frame as consequence, not punishment: "Failure becomes someone else's good week" is a stated fact about where the money goes, not a guilt trip.

## Pronouns (decided 2026-07-20)

- **"You / your group"** — default everywhere in product and marketing.
- **"We" — sparingly.** Allowed in About, support, and policy contexts where a first party must speak ("We charge $1 per forfeit; the rest stays with your group"). Avoid "we" in product UI and marketing headlines — there the product is named or silent.
- **"I"** — founder content only.

## Casing & punctuation

- **Sentence case** for headlines and body. Never title case.
- **UPPERCASE tracked mono** only for kickers, labels, meta rows: `VOL. 01 · CYCLE INFRASTRUCTURE`.
- **Middle dot (·)** separates meta facts instead of commas or pipes: "4 members · daily cadence · $25 at-stake."
- **Italics** (serif) carry thematic weight inside an otherwise plain sentence: "Built for groups, on a *cycle*."
- **No emoji.** Anywhere. Ever.
- **No em dashes.** Too recognizable as AI-generated. Use periods, colons, or semicolons instead.
- **Hashtags** (social posts only): **1–3**, niche over broad, on their own line at the end of the post — never mid-sentence, never stuffed. House defaults: `#Accountability`, `#FollowThrough`, `#AccountabilityGroup`; swap in a theme tag (e.g. `#Discipline`) when the post's angle calls for it.

## Headline framing (added 2026-09-18)

**No question-mark titles.** Article H1s, title tags, and social hooks should be assertive statements, not questions — a Stanford study (July 2026, 22K+ news articles, 3M+ academic articles, 53K Reddit posts) found question-framed titles get 71.6% fewer comments and 12.6% fewer clicks than statement titles across all question types (direct, rhetorical, open-ended). Mechanism: a question signals uncertainty at the exact moment a reader is deciding whether the content is worth their time; a statement signals the writer already knows the answer.

- Prefer: "How to Start an Accountability Group That Actually Sticks," "Accountability Partner vs. Accountability Group: Why One Person Isn't Enough" — imperative, comparative, or declarative framing.
- Avoid: "Is an Accountability Partner Enough?", "Do You Need an Accountability Group?" — rewrite as the confident version ("Why One Person Isn't Enough," not "Is One Person Enough?").
- This is already how every title in the current keyword shortlist (`docs/marketing/blog-keyword-target-sheet-2026-09-15.md`) is framed — keep it that way going forward, don't drift into question titles for variety.
- **Exception: paid social ad headlines (Brandon, 2026-10-02).** Ad headlines on Reddit (and other paid social) may be questions. Reddit's 2026 Creative Best Practices study found conversational headlines, questions included, perform well in-feed, which conflicts with the Stanford finding above (whose dataset also included Reddit posts). Rather than pick a winner on paper, the Reddit test plan runs question vs. statement headlines head to head (`docs/marketing/reddit-ad-creative-plan-2026-10-01.md`, month 2). Revisit this exception once that result is in. Blog titles, title tags, and organic social hooks keep the no-question rule.
- **No humor in ad creative either.** The "fully straight" rule applies to Reddit and meme-native channels too; no comedic memes (Brandon, 2026-10-02).
- **Exception: a heading answering a real reader question can stay a literal question, wherever it sits in the article.** The research is about the click-through decision (a title competing for attention in a feed/SERP); a heading read by someone already on the page is a different situation, and the question form is load-bearing there — it mirrors real search queries for "people also ask" matching and is what `FAQPage` schema expects (`Question`/`acceptedAnswer` pairs). This applies to any H2/H3 answering a real query, not only inside a section literally labeled "FAQ" (as of 2026-09-19, Field notes articles no longer use a dedicated FAQ block — see `blog-article-structure` skill; the same question-heading exception still applies wherever those questions now live in the normal section flow).

## Field notes: reader-first language and personal angles (added 2026-09-19)

- **Write for someone who has never heard of Accountability Guild.** Field notes have to be valuable to a reader who never uses the product. Headings, table headers, the Quick Summary, and the first sentence of a paragraph use ordinary words. Product terms from the glossary ("cycle," "proof," "forfeit," "kitty," "hall pass," "standard," "report," "close") belong only in an optional closing sentence of a paragraph or section, after the general idea has been explained in general terms. Example: not "Log proof as you go, not just at the close," but "Record your progress as you go instead of remembering it at the end of the month," with "Accountability Guild calls this logging proof" (if it fits) at the end of the paragraph.
- **Headings state the section's main point.** Prefer a statement a skimmer can learn from ("A monthly rhythm works for busy people, with two conditions") over a topic label or a question. This narrows the earlier question-heading exception: use a question heading only when a real reader query is the clearest match and no statement does the job.
- **Open with a person, not a manual.** Every Field notes article has an introduction after the Quick Summary. Where it honestly can, it opens with a relatable feeling or situation or a real personal angle. Never invent an anecdote, a member, a number, or a result. Confirmed real story: Brandon Tilus built Accountability Guild for his own group, which meets on the first Thursday of every month (keep that in third person under Grant's byline).
- **Always close with the takeaway.** Every article ends with a section whose heading states the primary takeaway and whose paragraph summarizes what the reader learned and encourages one specific next step they can take with or without Accountability Guild.
- **Open question for Grant:** "I" is currently founder content only (Brandon). Field notes are bylined Grant Tilus. Until this is decided, Field notes use relatable second-person or observational angles and third-person references to Brandon; a first-person anecdote is used only if Grant supplies a real one, and this rule gets reconciled then.

## "AG" shorthand (decided 2026-07-20)

"AG" is acceptable **internally** and in **Field notes / blog** content after the full name is established. **Never** in core website copy, product UI, or first mention — there it's always "Accountability Guild."

## Quick do / don't

- Do: "You committed to three workouts. You logged two. $25 to the kitty."
- Don't: "Oops — looks like you missed one! 😅 Time to feed the kitty!"
- Do: "Built for groups, on a cycle. Receipts at the close."
- Don't: "Supercharge your team's accountability journey today!"
