1. Everyone sets their goals for the cycle.

The group’s organizer sets the schedule and the stakes, then invites the group. Everyone else joins from an email invite, writes their own measurable goals (called commitments), and agrees to the group’s schedule and stakes before the first cycle starts.

3. Report back to the group before you meet.

A report is a short check-in at the end of each cycle: score each goal and write a few lines on what happened. Your whole group reads it, so they know where you’re stuck, can push you when you slip, and back you when you’re close.

4. Missed goals add money to the group’s shared pot.

Each member puts money on the line for the cycle, which adds one more layer of accountability. At the end, you’re charged for the goals you missed, and the money collects in the group’s kitty. The organizer pays it out for whatever the group agreed, and everyone sees every withdrawal.

Put money on your goals. Misses pay your group, not Accountability Guild.

Every member puts the same amount at stake each cycle, say $100, divided evenly across their own goals. At the end of the cycle, you pay only for the goals you missed, or the part of a goal you didn’t finish. Miss nothing, pay nothing.

Each member’s amounts come from their own goals. No report means the full $100.

When is my card charged?

Each member saves a card when they join the group. It’s charged automatically at the end of the cycle, and only for the goals, or parts of goals, you missed. Hit all your goals and you pay nothing.

Where does the money go?

Into your group’s kitty, for whatever the group agrees on: a retreat or a group dinner, a payout to whoever kept the most goals, a shared subscription, or a charity. Accountability Guild keeps $1 per charge, and card processing comes out of the same amount.

How is the kitty spent?

The organizer pays it out once the group agrees on a use. Every withdrawal shows the amount and the reason to the whole group.

Why don’t you charge a monthly fee?

The point is for you and your group to hit your goals, so there’s nothing to pay up front. Accountability Guild makes money only when a forfeit is collected: a $1 platform fee plus card processing, both taken out of the forfeit, not added on top.

Built for a first-Thursday group that still runs on it.

I built this because I’m part of a small group that meets on the first Thursday of each month, and the reporting rhythm should not depend on one person chasing everyone. The app I wanted didn’t exist: every habit tracker is built for one person, alone.

Accountability Guild is a different shape. It assumes the room. It assumes you’ve already done the hard part: finding three or five or ten people who agreed to hold each other to something.

One cycle, then the next. Receipts at the close. That’s it.
