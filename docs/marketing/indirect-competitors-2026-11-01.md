**Evidence tags** (same as the scorer): ✓ sourced (vendor page or published research), ◦ secondary (third-party, vendor-adjacent, anecdote), ▪ internal (our docs), ✗ unverified (inference, no source).

**Method notes**
- Prices checked 2026-11-01. Where the vendor page wouldn't load (Practice, WIP, stickK 403, Habitica, Strava, Splitwise showed no price), the figure is from a third-party listing and tagged secondary.

## Deep dives (the 10 most common)

**Group chat.** Better than us: already installed, it's where the group talks, there's no setup, and posting a proof photo is one tap. Lacks: a record of commitments, cycle state, a tally of misses, money. Adoption: WhatsApp has 3 billion monthly users (Statista, Oct 2025) ◦; GroupMe groups now allow 10,000+ members (GroupMe 2025 review) ✓; GroupMe's EU monthly recipients were about 202k (Microsoft DSA disclosure) ◦. Switching cost: very high. Groups won't leave their chat, so the product should post into it rather than replace it ✗.

**Free sheet trackers.** Better: unlimited flexibility; the owner controls the format and the penalty math. Lacks: they're built for one person, so group use means hand updates, with no reminders, proof or charging. Adoption: no download counts found, and the Microsoft and Google galleries weren't checked ✗. Switching: medium. The owner built it.

**Notion / Gumroad mastermind templates.** Natascha Buck's Mastermind Group template ($4.99+) has a meeting calendar, member contacts and an accountability list ✓. Tanya Moushi's facilitator dashboard is pay-what-you-want ✓. Cohorty's $12 partner tracker says it isn't meant for groups of 3+ ✓. Lacks: static pages with no close and no money. Sales counts aren't shown ✗. Switching: low.

**Slack and Discord.** Better: channels, bots and stand-up threads. Lacks: commitment records, and free Slack keeps only 90 days of history, so past cycles disappear ✓. Adoption among accountability groups: no data ✗. Switching: high for the community, low for the ritual inside it.

**Habitica.** Better: a member's missed dailies damage the whole party, a social consequence with no money. Parties and challenges are free ◦. Lacks: game framing, no report, no real money. Adoption: no current official count ✗.

**Skool.** $9/mo plus a 10% fee, or $99/mo plus 2.9% ✓. About 42,100 communities tracked, 12,500 of them paid (Profitable.app estimate) ◦. Better: community, courses, calendar and paid membership in one place. Lacks: commitments, close, forfeits. Switching: high, because it holds the facilitator's paying members.

**Circle.** $89 / $199 / $419 a month, billed annually ✓. Circle's own customer community has 27,000+ members ✓. Same gaps as Skool.

**CoachAccountable.** $20/mo for 2 clients, up to $4,000/mo for 1,000; each person in a group is a separate client ✓. Better: built for coaches tracking client commitments. Lacks: peer visibility and a kitty; per-client billing makes groups expensive. Feature details weren't confirmed on the page fetched ✗.

**Venmo Groups.** Launched November 2023 to track, split and settle shared costs; reported limit of 30 members ◦. It's the payment rail peer groups already use. Nothing links a request to a missed commitment.

**stickK.** Group campaigns from free to $99/mo (business evaluation, May 2026; the vendor page returned 403 today) ◦. An academic dataset covers 742,923 users and 397,456 commitments (ICWSM 2024) ✓. Better: referees confirm reports. Lacks: contracts are individual, with no shared cycle or kitty.

## Answers

**Which of the four acts does nobody cover well?** Pooling the forfeits. Only DietBet and StepBet pool money, for fitness games the company runs, and the pot goes to the winners rather than the group. Venmo Groups and Splitwise settle debts with no link to commitments. Close with receipts comes second: full closes exist only in individual stakes apps and in live, leader-run meetings.

**Which free substitute is hardest to displace, and why?** The group chat. It's already installed, it's where the group lives, and it loosely covers three acts. The product should work alongside it, posting reminders, close summaries and kitty totals into the chat, not compete with it ✗. The owner's own Google Sheet comes second for owner-led groups.

**Does any segment's ranking change?** Yes. With the applied scores and equal weights:
- Church groups fall from tied 3rd (3.50) to tied 4th with fitness (3.25).
- Group coaches fall to 7th on their own (2.75 → 2.50).
- Peer circles and mastermind facilitators stay tied at the top (3.75), and solo founders move to sole 3rd (3.50).
